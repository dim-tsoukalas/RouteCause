2. Definition of Route Leak
A route leak is the propagation of routing announcements beyond their intended
scope. That is, an announcement from an autonomous system (AS) of a learned
BGP route to another AS is in violation of the intended policies of the
receiver, the sender, and/or one of the ASes along the preceding AS path. The
intended scope is usually defined by a set of local redistribution and
filtering policies distributed among the ASes involved. Route leaks can lead to
traffic being redirected through unintended paths, enabling eavesdropping or
traffic analysis, and can cause overload or blackholing.

3. Classification of Route Leaks
Type 1 (Hairpin): A multihomed AS learns a route from one upstream and leaks it
to another upstream. Type 2 (Lateral ISP-ISP-ISP leak). Type 4 (Leak of
Provider Prefixes to Peer). Type 6 (Leak of Peer Prefixes to Provider). In each
type, a prefix appears via an AS path that violates the valley-free / no-valley
routing policy expected between providers, peers, and customers.

4. Origin and Hijack Considerations
A prefix normally originated by a single AS may appear originated by a
different AS (a Multiple Origin AS, MOAS, condition). While some MOAS cases are
legitimate (e.g., anycast, multi-homing with multiple origins), an unexpected
change in origin AS for a prefix is a strong indicator of a prefix hijack or
misconfiguration and warrants validation against RPKI Route Origin
Authorizations (ROAs) and IRR data.
